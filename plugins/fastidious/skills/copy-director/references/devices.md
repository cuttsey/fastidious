# Copy devices

The devices the copy director reaches for, with when each works and how each fails. Examples are invented for a fictional cider maker, Hollow Lane, so nothing here can be lifted onto a real site.

## Devices

| Device | What it is | Works when | Fails when | Example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Antithesis | Two halves set against each other | The position is defined by what the category is not | Both halves are vague, or the "against" is a straw man | "Pressed in autumn, poured in spring." |
| Three-beat claim (tricolon) | Three short parallel statements | Carrying a method or a model, one beat per scene | The third beat is filler, or the three are not parallel | "We pick it. We press it. We wait." |
| Anaphora | Repeating the opening of successive lines | Building rhythm across beats | Repeated past three, or used on every section | "We pick by hand. We press by weight. We sell by the season." |
| The turn | A familiar phrase changed at the end | The change carries the idea | The original phrase is too obscure to hear | "Good things come to those who wait. Better cider too." |
| The name | A line that makes the name mean the promise | The name truly carries it | It is a pun the reader groans at, or used twice | "Hollow Lane: the long way round, on purpose." |
| Specific number | A figure with its unit and its source | It is checkable and surprising | It is rounded, unsourced or decorative | "Eleven months in oak." |
| Concrete list | Three to five named things instead of a category | Replacing words like "solutions" or "everything you need" | It becomes a feature dump | "Kingston Black, Dabinett, Yarlington Mill." |
| Disclosure aside | A short, labelled aside with a term a cautious reader wants | Ownership, limits, what happens if it fails | It hides a catch in small type | "Plainly: we sell out most years. If we do, you are first in line next spring." |
| Disqualification | Saying who the offer is not for | The exclusion is real and situational | It insults the reader, or excludes no one | "Not for parties of fifty. We make 4,000 bottles a year." |
| Objection order | FAQs in the order a sceptic raises doubts | Each answer leads with the answer | The order follows the org chart instead of the reader | "Is it sweet? No, it is dry, and here is why." |
| Callback | The close answers or repeats the opening | The spine is strong enough to carry it | The page never earned the return | Opening and closing on the same spine line |
| Understatement | Saying less than the work shows | The pictures or proof are strong | There is nothing behind it | "It takes a while." |

## Display lines

- Write headings with their breaks. Break at a natural pause, keep the last line shorter than the first, and never leave one short word alone on a line.
- Keep heroes to eight words or fewer. A second line may carry the position in a quieter register.
- One device per line. Antithesis and a pun in the same heading cancel each other out.

## Shapes the lint fails

- **The "not X, it's Y" shape** and its relatives ("it isn't just X, it's Y", "not only X but Y", "more than just X"). Say what the thing is.
- **Em dashes**, in any position. Use a colon, a comma or a full stop.

## Shapes the lint warns on

- Sentences over 25 words, and display lines over eight words.
- Headings in Title Case.
- Exclamation marks.
- Flesch reading ease below 50 for body text (Flesch, 1948). It is a rough guide for English and is reported, not enforced.
