# What makes a brand distinctive: lessons from seven studios

Studied in a browser, 2026-10-30, at laptop and desktop widths: Pink Pony Creative, CJ Cawley, Primary, Beach, Pentagram, Kingdom & Sparrow, Geist*Studio. Some hero films didn't load in the browser pane (CJ Cawley, Beach, Kingdom & Sparrow first screens), so these rest on the rest of each page. Take the principle, never the specifics.

## What they do
| Studio | The commitment | How it's carried out |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pink Pony | The name, literally | An actual glossy pink horse, the team dressed in pink and black beside it, the team called "the cowgirls", hot pink fields, then "Never beige. Never background. Never safe." |
| CJ Cawley | A rebel with a hand-drawn attitude | A mischievous mark, "Designing badass brands for the weird & wonderful", black ground that lets loud work shout, "Fortune favours the bold" |
| Primary | One claim about speed | "Blazing fast brand sprints", "Zero to full brand in two weeks", acid lime, a timeline as a device. "Your journey is short because ours was long." |
| Beach | Play, as a belief | "Good design comes from the most playful places"; a chunky gold 3D mark in the footer; a Play page |
| Pentagram | Range, as a sentence you operate | "We design everything for everyone", with both nouns as menus; the work does all the talking |
| Kingdom & Sparrow | Artistry and craft | Bold round caps in hand-drawn-feeling pills, rich product photography; a real awards claim |
| Geist*Studio | Considered and bold, at once | Italic-roman serif headline, mono labels and real people in a real room; "Good work comes from understanding people" |

## What generalises
1. **Commit to one thing past the point of good sense.** The distance past sensible is where the personality lives. A tasteful version of the idea has no personality.
2. **Perform the name literally.** Then let the people, the copy and the footer play along.
3. **Own one colour or one material, loudly.** Not a tasteful dark ground with restraint. People should be able to name the colour afterwards.
4. **Have something to be for, and an enemy.** A single sentence anyone can repeat: "Never beige", "Blazing fast", "Everything for everyone".
5. **Warmth, not irony.** All seven like their clients and enjoy the work. They're proud and inviting. Superior wit from a distance isn't on this list.
6. **Claims come with a number or a name.** "Two weeks." "Two decades at Pentagram." Plain, checkable, unembarrassed about selling.
7. **Real work, large, early.** Variety in the work shows range, while the brand stays constant around it.
8. **People and hands are visible.** Faces, a team, a room or a drawn character. A brand is someone.
9. **One signature asset.** The horse, the mark, the gold logo. You could recognise the brand from that one object.
10. **House-style rules don't make personality.** Geist has mono labels and index numbers, and an italic word in the headline. Pink Pony numbers its services and uses a logo row. These pages break every one of Taste's bans and are more distinctive for it. The bans prevent average work. They can't create a brand, and they can flatten one. When a brand's idea needs a banned device, the idea wins.

## My own tells, to watch for
Restraint offered as taste. Dark, tasteful grounds. Irony instead of warmth. Concept over commitment. Treating "nothing wrong" as a result.
