# Motifs and illustration

## Finding a motif
Start from the sign inventory in `SIGN-SYSTEM.md`. Prefer **indexical** signs: marks physically caused by the subject. They are hard to fake and belong to nobody else.

| Kind | Examples | Becomes |
|---|---|---|
| Marks the activity leaves | a chalk line, a footprint, a grass stain, a ring of a cup, a tide line, a tyre track | dividers, progress, section edges, cursors |
| Marks of making | a stitch, a stamp, a brush stroke, a weld bead, a saw kerf, a printer's register mark | hover gestures, underlines, badges |
| Units and counters of the world | pins, rounds, sets, knots, rows, tally marks | progress meters, ratings, bullets |
| Shapes of the place | a coastline, a field boundary, a roofline, a river's course | frames, section breaks, backgrounds |
| Objects and tools | the jacket, the bell, the ticket, the label | spot illustrations, empty states |

Test each candidate:
1. **Competitor test:** could the nearest competitor use it? Then drop it.
2. **Insider test:** would a practitioner find it right in detail?
3. **Stranger test:** does it read, or have an anchoring caption?

## Rules for a motif system
- **Two or three motifs, no more.** Each has a meaning, jobs, a scale range and a density limit.
- **Repetition with variation:** the same mark at small (a bullet), medium (a divider or progress) and large (a hero frame or empty state).
- **Density:** usually one or two per screen. A motif on every element is wallpaper.
- **Behaviour:** if a motif moves, the motion comes from what the real thing does: a line is drawn, a stamp presses, a pin goes down.
- **Never:** behind body text at a contrast that harms reading; as the only carrier of meaning; outside its scale range.

## Illustration
| Modality | Claims | Use for | Style notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Documentary (photo, archive) | "this happened" | evidence, history, people | never generated or retouched beyond correction; credited |
| Diagrammatic | "this is how it works" | techniques, processes, rules | icon stroke ×2–3; colour roles only; labels in text |
| Expressive | "this is how it feels" | heroes, campaigns, empty states | one illustrator's hand; the palette; no stock scenes |

- Spot illustrations use the icon grid at 2–4× scale and the same stroke logic, so they sit with the icons.
- Illustrated people: one consistent style. Avoid the generic flat style (blobby people, tiny heads, purple skin), which signals a template.
- AI imagery is expressive at most. Label it, and never caption it as real people, events or members.

## Worked example (a traditional wrestling society)
- **Need test:** words for navigation and actions. Convention icons for close, menu and external link only. The → arrow on actions. **Encoded marks** (pins, filled or open dots) for skill and strength levels and for progress. No feature icons.
- **Motifs:**
  1. *The chalk ring* (indexical: the ground marking) for frames, the cursor and section ends.
  2. *The four pins* (the sport's unit) for progress, levels and bullets.
  3. *Mown stripes* (the field) for grounds only.
- **Illustration:** documentary archive prints, credited; diagrammatic technique drawings to come (a pictogram set is an open decision); expressive cut-out figures, labelled as illustration.
- **Refused:** fists, crossed swords, trophies, shields, lightbulbs.
