# Staging an idea as scenes

An allegory shows an abstract idea as people and things doing something. Renaissance and Baroque artists worked from manuals such as Cesare Ripa's *Iconologia* (1593), which gave each virtue, art or quality a figure, a costume and attributes, so viewers could read the idea from the picture. A brand world can do the same: each claim the business makes becomes an act in one world, performed by a recurring cast.

## From claim to act

For each claim in `COPY-DECK.md`:

1. **Name the verb.** What does the business actually do to make this claim true? Build, measure, protect, wait, choose, repair.
2. **Find the act in the world.** Translate the verb into a physical task native to the chosen world: in a Renaissance workshop, building is raising a statue; in a botanical world, it is grafting; in a railway world, laying track.
3. **Choose the object that changes.** The through-line object should change state in a way that matches the claim: green to gold, rough to finished, seed to fruit, blank map to drawn map.
4. **Cast it.** Who performs the act, and who watches? Keep the cast small and recurring.
5. **Write the one-line scene.** "A gardener binds a cutting to an old branch with twine." If it takes two sentences, the scene has two acts and should be two scenes.

## Reading at three levels

Erwin Panofsky (1939) described three levels of meaning in a picture. Use them as a check on every scene card.

| Level | Question | A scene passes when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pre-iconographic: what is shown | What do you literally see? | Anyone can describe it: people, objects, an action |
| Iconographic: the conventional meaning | What does this act or object usually stand for? | The convention points the right way (gold for value, scaffolding for construction, a seedling for a beginning) |
| Iconological: what it says here | What principle about this business does it reveal? | The person the work is for recognises their own situation in it |

If a scene only works at the third level, it is a private joke. If it only works at the first, it is decoration.

## Shape of the sequence

- **Open on the promise** made visible: the finished thing, or its first sign.
- **Middle on the work:** the acts that make the promise true, one claim each, in the order the business does them.
- **Turn on the terms:** the moment value changes hands, shown as an act (sharing, weighing, signing, harvesting).
- **Close by answering the opening:** the cycle restarting, the object complete, the cast at rest. A close that echoes the opening tells the visitor the story is finished.

## Devices from outside the world

A modern instrument inside an old world (a prismatic tool, print dots, a survey grid) can carry the part of the claim the world cannot: technology, measurement, visibility. Each device:

- has exactly one job, written in the bible;
- looks the same every time it appears;
- appears where that job is in the story, and nowhere else.
