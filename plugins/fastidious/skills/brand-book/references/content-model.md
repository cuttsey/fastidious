# Brand book content model

This is the content a complete digital brand book carries. It is generalised from studying mature brand portals: Bang & Olufsen's "Brand identity" document, whose chapters are Brand position, Tone of voice, Visual identity, Colours, Imagery, Wordmark & Logomark, Typography, Layouts, Music and sound design, Brand codes, Iconography, Applications and Downloads, along with its separate partner, print and product documents. Use the chapters in this order unless the brand's own logic suggests otherwise. **Required** items must appear, either as content or as a "To decide" gap. **Optional** items appear when the brand has them.

## Page anatomy (every page)
1. **Purpose line.** One sentence saying what the page lets you do ("Choose the right roundel for any background").
2. **The rule.** Short, imperative, numbered if there is more than one.
3. **The specimen.** The real thing, shown: swatch, type, logo on its ground, image, screenshot.
4. **Dos and don'ts.** Paired, visual where possible, each with a one-line reason.
5. **Why.** One or two sentences linking the rule to the brand's position or personality.
6. **Status.** Locked / recommendation / to decide, with the source file.

## 0. Cover and how to use this book (required)
- The brand's name and the book's title, version, date and owner.
- Who the book is for, and how to find things (chapter index, linking to sections).
- A one-line summary of the brand idea.

## 1. Brand position (required)
- **Position line:** the one sentence the brand stands on, set large. B&O pairs it with an inverted echo ("B&O is beautiful sound. Beautiful sound is B&O.").
- **Brand statement:** a short manifesto paragraph in the brand voice, saying what it believes, what it rejects and what it does.
- **The shifts:** "from → to" pairs naming how the brand must change the way it shows up ("To win, we must make shifts").
- **Audience:** who it is for, their need, and what they should feel or do.
- **Personality:** three to five traits, each with what it permits and rules out.
- **The myth or core idea**, with the evidence that supports it (optional but strong).
- **Next steps:** how the position will be operationalised (optional).

## 2. Tone of voice (required)
- **Voice principles:** three to five, each with a do/don't sentence pair.
- **How the voice flexes** by context: headline, instruction, error, social, formal (optional).
- **Lexicon:** words we use (with glosses for any jargon or dialect) and words we avoid, with reasons.
- **Microcopy patterns:** calls to action, confirmations, errors, the 404 and the sign-off.
- **Writing mechanics:** spelling standard, case, numbers, dates, punctuation (recommendation if not sourced).

## 3. Visual identity overview (required)
- The identity's parts on one page (logo, colour, type, imagery, codes), with how they work together and the single idea they serve.

## 4. Logo: wordmark and logomark (required)
- **Overview:** each mark and what it is for (symbol, wordmark, lockups).
- **Versions:** colour, mono dark, mono light, and when to use each.
- **Clear space and minimum size,** for digital and print.
- **Placement and backgrounds:** which version on which ground and on photographs (a cheat sheet table).
- **Misuse:** don'ts such as redrawing, stretching, recolouring, effects, busy grounds or rotating.
- **Lockups:** with the name, with sub-brands or clubs, and **third-party/partner lockups** (spacing, divider, size parity).
- **Downloads:** the real files.

## 5. Colour (required)
- **Signature colour(s):** the one(s) people recognise the brand by.
- **Foundation colours:** grounds and ink.
- **Secondary or tertiary colours:** fields and accents, with their jobs.
- **Values:** HEX, RGB, token name, and **CMYK or Pantone** for print (a gap if absent).
- **Proportion:** how much of each, shown as a bar.
- **Schemes:** approved combinations of ground, text and accent.
- **Typographic colour:** which text colours are allowed on which grounds, with contrast ratios and pass/fail.
- **Utility colours:** focus, links, success, warning, error, info.
- **Data colours:** chart palette and order (a gap if absent).
- **Don'ts:** for example, accent as text where it fails contrast.

## 6. Typography (required)
- **Typefaces:** each face, its role, why it was chosen, its licence and where to get it.
- **Specimen and glyphs:** the alphabet, figures and key characters.
- **Typeface settings:** width, weight, case, tracking and leading per role.
- **Hierarchy:** the type scale with tokens and sizes from phone to desktop.
- **Text lockups:** how headline, standfirst and action sit together.
- **Expressive or rhythmic typography:** any signature type behaviour (optional).
- **Dos and don'ts.**
- **Back-up fonts:** system fallbacks, and the font to use in Office or email.

## 7. Imagery (required)
- **Imagery idea:** a three-word line (B&O: "Striking. Intimate. Profound.").
- **Image types:** for each one (emotive, product or practice, place, people, archive or illustration), the purpose, examples, dos and don'ts.
- **Treatment:** colour grading, crops, cut-outs and shadows, what is never done.
- **People and styling:** casting, clothing and diversity (optional, or a gap).
- **Credits and rights:** how to caption and credit; what needs permission.

## 8. Brand codes (required)
- The few signature signs that make the brand recognisable without the logo. B&O calls theirs a "four-part harmony" of colour, aluminium, wood and the circle. For each code, give its meaning, how to use it and how not to.

## 9. Layout (required)
- Margins, gutters, columns and section rhythm (tokens).
- Grids for portrait and landscape formats, print and digital.
- Composition principles, with examples.
- Templates: presentation, social, poster and print (a gap if absent).

## 10. Motion and interaction (required for digital brands)
- Easing and duration tokens, and what the motion means.
- Signature interactions: cursor, hover gesture, scrollbar, meters.
- Reduced-motion rules.

## 11. Sound (optional; a gap if the brand uses video or events)
- Music and sound principles, the sonic logo, interface sounds, and licensing.

## 12. Iconography (optional or a gap)
- Icon style, grid, stroke, and the set, or the rule that the brand uses none.

## 13. Applications (required)
- **In the world (required, built with `supermock`):** moving mockups. These are the site on a laptop and a phone, out-of-home pinned into real credited photographs, animated display ads and a showreel, each captioned with format, place and what it shows. Placements are labelled as concepts.
- The identity in use: website, merchandise, print, social, signage and documents. Each one shows what it demonstrates.

## 14. Downloads (required)
- Logos, tokens or colour files, fonts (or links), templates and image packs, each with its format and when to use it.

## 15. Governance (required)
- Owner and contact, version, change log, and how to request a new asset or exception.
- **Open decisions:** the "To decide" list, mirrored in `BRAND-BOOK-GAPS.md`.

## Partner and sub-brand documents (optional)
Mature portals keep separate documents for partners, print, product and professional channels. For smaller brands, a "Working with partners and clubs" page covering lockups, co-branding and what partners may do is enough.
